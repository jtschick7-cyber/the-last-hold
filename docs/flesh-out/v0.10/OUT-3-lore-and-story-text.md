**Chapter conditions beyond level and seat** (a chapter that is due but not playable waits; it is never lost). A chapter is **playable** when its Order is open and seated, the Order's level is at least the chapter's number, the previous chapter is read, and its condition below holds. An unseated Order's waiting chapter **does not block** another Order's chapter.

## Part C — Opening scenes (one each; a scene sheet, kept in the Library's Orders shelf)

##### OS-M · A seat at the table *(the Thanes' Moot opens)*
> Brana comes up from the gate with a folded paper and the look she keeps for bad weather.
>
> "Marta Dubska sent this by the ferry boy. It isn't a notice. It's an invitation, which is worse, because you have to answer it." She sets it down. "The five heads want a table with the hold at it. Not a council, she says. A habit."
>
> Pechko, from behind the stove: "Habits are how you tell a house from a heap of stones."
>
> Brana sniffs, and goes to see about the chairs.

##### OS-G · A basket on the step *(the Grey Wives open)*
> Someone has left a basket on the Hut step, under a cloth. Dried yarrow tied with red thread. A twist of honeycomb. A small card with a kettle drawn on it and five words in a careful hand: *For whoever keeps these kettles.*
>
> Brana turns the card over. Nothing on the back. "Herbwives, by the knot," she says. "They call themselves the Grey Wives."
>
> Pechko, from the doorway: "Leave the thread on. It's tied that way for a reason."

##### OS-B · Who keeps the bells *(the Bell-Watchers open)*
> Ilse sets a small pile of letters on the Library table and squares the edges.
>
> "Four families have written to ask whether the hold keeps a bell. Not for a service. They ring for the ones who went onward, they say, and they'd like to know whether anyone at Stanholm does." She taps the top letter. "They call themselves the Bell-Watchers. I hadn't heard it before. Everyone I asked says it has always been so."
>
> "Has it?"
>
> "Nobody I asked could say when it began." She squares the pile again. "That is its own kind of answer."

##### OS-Q · Named for the metal *(the Quenchers open)*
> Brana puts Věra's letter down beside a second one that has no signature at all.
>
> "Kozly Hollow's young ones have given themselves a name," she says. "They call themselves the Quenchers."
>
> "Quenchers," Ilse says, and thinks. "To cool hot metal. Quickly, in water. So it can be handled."
>
> "Or so it stops," says Pechko, who has been listening and pretending not to.
>
> Nobody answers him.

## Part D — The sixteen chapters

Each is a scene sheet, about the length of the Act VI closing scenes. Chapter *n* plays when the Order's standing reaches level *n* **and** the Order holds a seat. Chapters are kept in the Library's **Orders** shelf and can be re-read.

### The Thanes' Moot

##### OR-M1 · A table at the gate
> Brana has put a long table under the gatehouse arch, which is not where she keeps tables. Five chairs on the long sides. The two ends are bare.
>
> Marta Dubska arrives first and counts the chairs twice. Gunnar Ravnsson comes up from the fell road with fish scales on his sleeve. Ilona Sváteková has brought a ledger and does not open it. Sólveig Arnadóttir sits with her hands folded over a small cloth bundle. Bohdan Kozel takes the chair nearest the door and says nothing at all.
>
> "I'll say it plain," Marta says. "Will the hearth still be lit in the spring?"
>
> You tell her it will.
>
> "Then tell the others."
>
> You do. Gunnar nods at the table. Ilona writes one line and shuts the book. Sólveig does not move.
>
> Brana puts out bread. Nobody takes an end chair. When the meal is over, the ends of the table are still bare, and nobody mentions it.

*Wrongness:* nobody sits at the head of the table.

##### OR-M2 · Salt and a ledger
> The heads have come back. Gunnar has brought his complaint in the form of a fish, which he lays on the table.
>
> "Ravenfell's barrow road has gone bad," he says. "Send a hunter up there before the snow."
>
> "Mirograd's mill road has been bad for a month," Ilona says. "Markets don't wait for barrows."
>
> Marta looks from one to the other. "The hold has hunters. It hasn't got a dozen."
>
> Sólveig reaches into her bundle, takes a pinch of salt, and sets it on the table between the fish and the ledger. She says nothing. Everyone looks at it.
>
> "That's a yes," Gunnar says at last. "Or a no."
>
> "It's salt," says Ilona.
>
> They settle it over the bread: one hunter to the barrow road, one to the mill road, and the ferry will wait its turn. Bohdan, who has not spoken, takes the last piece of bread and eats it standing.

*Wrongness:* nobody can say what the salt means, and nobody asks Sólveig.

##### OR-M3 · The one who leaves
> Today the argument is about the grain store. Marta wants it kept at Dubno Ford, where the ferry can reach it. Gunnar wants it at Velkvik, because the fells always run short first. Ilona has a number for each, and it is not a number either of them likes.
>
> It is Gunnar who says it: "There's a wall of names in your gatehouse. Open, and written down, so nobody can say a thing was forgotten."
>
> "The Roll of Honor," Marta says.
>
> Ilona closes her ledger and stands. She looks at no one. She goes out through the gatehouse arch, and the door does not bang, because she closes it with her hand flat against the wood.
>
> Nobody speaks for a while. Then Marta says, "Mirograd's vote goes where mine goes," and rules the grain store into two halves, one at the Ford and one at Velkvik.
>
> Ilona's place at the bread stays empty. Bohdan looks at her chair as if it might explain itself.

*Wrongness:* Ilona is the only head who does not say why she left.

##### OR-M4 · What is it for? *(two endings)*
> The five heads have come to ask one question, and they have written it down so that none of them can change it. Marta reads it out.
>
> "What is the hold for?"
>
> It is a shorter question than you expected, and a longer one than it looks.

**Variant A (the Quenchers are also seated):**
> You have barely begun when the door opens. Bohdan Kozel is standing in it.
>
> He has not come to sit. He stands, and he says that Kozly Hollow will listen to the answer and will not vote on it.
>
> "I'm not a guest," he says. "I'm not a head tonight either. I'm somebody who would like to hear what you say."
>
> Marta moves her chair six inches to the left. Nobody remarks on it. You give your answer, and the heads listen to all of it.

**Variant B (the Quenchers are not seated):**
> You give your answer. The heads put it to a vote: Marta, Gunnar and Ilona raise a hand, and Sólveig lays a pinch of salt in front of her place. Kozly Hollow's chair stays empty. Nobody says Bohdan's name.

**Ending, both variants:**
> When it is over there is a sixth chair at the table. Brana brought it in without being asked. It stands at the head, and nobody sits in it.

*Wrongness:* the head of the table, bare in the first chapter, now has a chair that no one will take. *Threshold:* the chair.

### The Grey Wives

##### OR-G1 · The kettle test
> Vesna of the Weir comes into the Hut with a very old woman on her arm and sets her down on the stool by the first kettle without explanation.
>
> "She asked to see where it's done," Vesna says. "That's all I'm allowed to say."
>
> The old woman is small, grey from her shawl to her boots. She lifts the lid of every kettle in turn and breathes in. She says nothing about any brew. She does not look at the shelves.
>
> At the last kettle she stops, and speaks for the first time.
>
> "Which herb do you never use?"
>
> You think about it. It takes a while to find the answer, and you tell her. She nods as if she had known.
>
> "Use it once," she says, "and tell Vesna what happens."

*Wrongness:* she asks what you leave out, not what you use.

##### OR-G2 · Tea for a recipe
> The eldest Grey Wife has come back, and she has brought a recipe in her head and nothing else. She will trade it, she says, for a cup of plain tea, and she will say it once.
>
> Ilse has her pencil out before the kettle boils.
>
> "Not written," the old woman says. "Say it back to me until you can say it without the paper."
>
> Ilse's pencil stops. "It will be lost if I don't."
>
> "It has been carried by mouth for a long time. It hasn't been lost yet."
>
> Ilse puts the pencil down. She says it back, and says it back again. The old woman drinks her tea and corrects her twice.

*Ilse's note, in the Library under this chapter:* *"Recipe received, not recorded. I can recite it. This feels like an admission."*

*Wrongness:* the recipe may not be written down by anyone, and the one who carries it is not worried.

##### OR-G3 · Two hands
> Ostap and Vesna are arguing in the Hut, politely, the way two people argue who have each been right a good many times.
>
> "Set the bone first," Ostap says. "Then the tea, once the pain's gone quiet."
>
> "Tea first," says Vesna. "A frightened patient won't hold still for a splint."
>
> "A frightened patient won't keep tea down."
>
> The eldest Grey Wife sits between them with her hands folded. She has not said a word. She looks at Ostap's hands, then at Vesna's, then at Ostap's again.
>
> "Two hands," she says at last. "One to hold. One to give." She does not say whose is which.
>
> Ostap grunts. Vesna smiles into her cup. Neither of them concedes anything.

*Wrongness:* she will not say whose hand is whose.

##### OR-G4 · What can't be mended
> The eldest Grey Wife asks the question she has plainly come to ask.
>
> "What does the hold do for someone it can't mend?"
>
> Nobody answers quickly. Ostap looks at his hands. Vesna looks at the old woman.
>
> You say what you think. The old woman listens to all of it and does not say whether you were right.
>
> When she goes, she leaves a cup on the table by the kettles. It is full. It has not been given to anyone.
>
> "For whoever comes," Vesna says. "It's how it's done."
>
> The tea goes cold. Nobody pours it out.

*Threshold:* the cup set out for no one.

### The Bell-Watchers

##### OR-B1 · The second spring
> Two women stand at the gate in the grey before breakfast, each with a small hand-bell. Halina has come up from Mirograd. Dana Lisová has come from Kozly Hollow, and has walked all night.
>
> They ring once each. The notes are different, thin and a little sour. They stop at the same moment, as if they had agreed.
>
> Ilse, watching from the Library door, says nothing until they have gone in for tea. Then she says to you, quietly, "I asked each of them how often they ring. Halina said every second spring. Dana said every second spring."
>
> "Does it matter?"
>
> "Neither of them said why it isn't every spring." She has marked the page. "I don't think either of them has asked."

*Wrongness:* the families ring every second spring and have not asked why.

##### OR-B2 · A list of springs
> The families have started bringing Ilse their lists. Not names. She says they will not give her names; they give her springs.
>
> There is a sheet from Mirograd in Halina's hand and a sheet from Kozly Hollow in Dana's, and more come in over the week. Each is a column of years in the order's reckoning, one under another, and beside each year a village and nothing else.
>
> "They ring on the springs when a child went up," Ilse says. "Look. Every year on every sheet is an even number."
>
> She runs her finger down the first column. "Nobody told them to keep to evens. They don't seem to have noticed."
>
> She puts a small pin in the corner of the sheet, the way she marks a thing she means to come back to.

*Wrongness:* every spring on every list is an even year, and nobody had noticed.

##### OR-B3 · What he said at the door
> Ilse has begun asking a different question. Not where they went, which nobody can answer, but what they said at the door.
>
> Halina: "He asked me not to cry at the gate. I cried at the gate." Dana: "He said he'd bring me a buckle." The others come in one at a time over the week: something about a dog, something about a roof, a joke that doesn't survive being written down. Ilse writes them all, carefully, on the backs of the lists.
>
> One sheet comes back blank. A note is pinned to it in someone else's hand: *We have stopped ringing. Please don't ask us to start.*
>
> Ilse sits with it for a long time.
>
> "I thought that would be the hard one," she says to you. "It's the kind one. They did it for as long as they could, and then they didn't."

*Wrongness:* one family has stopped, and asks not to be asked.

##### OR-B4 · A bell of our own
> Halina and Dana Lisová have come up together this time. They have brought nothing in their hands.
>
> "We would like to ask something," Halina says. Dana is looking at the stones.
>
> "A bell," says Dana. "At the gatehouse. Not to ring for anyone in particular. Just there."
>
> Ilse starts to say something about records, and doesn't.
>
> It takes Brana and two peasants an afternoon to hang it from the gatehouse arch, on an iron hook that was already in the stone. It is a small bell of dull bronze, and its clapper is wrapped in cloth.
>
> Halina reaches up and unwraps it. Then she lets go of the cloth, and she doesn't ring it.
>
> "Not yet," she says.

*Threshold:* the bell hangs, unrung.

### The Quenchers

##### OR-Q1 · Three at the gate
> Bohdan Kozel is at the gate with three young people behind him, goatherds by their boots. He takes his cap off, which in Kozly Hollow is a great deal.
>
> He says it plainly: "We'd like to see the stove. Only see it."
>
> Brana looks at you. You nod.
>
> Pechko is out from behind it before they have crossed the Hall. He sits on his stool with the broom across his knees like a gatekeeper, says nothing, and lets them look.
>
> The three stand at a polite distance and look at the stove for a long time. One of them holds out a hand, palm open, to feel the heat. She takes it back quickly.
>
> "It's warm," she says, as if somebody had told her otherwise.
>
> "Aye," says Pechko.
>
> They thank him, which few people do, and Pechko, to his own surprise, tells them to mind the step.

*Wrongness:* they are polite, and they are afraid of a warm stove.

##### OR-Q2 · Věra's chair
> Věra has come up from the Hollow on a goat cart, and she sits where she can see both Bohdan and the stove.
>
> "Hear the warden out," she tells him. "Then say your piece. In that order."
>
> "You won't stand with us," Bohdan says.
>
> "I wrote what I wrote, and I meant it. That doesn't make me one of you. Sit down."
>
> He doesn't sit. He counts on his fingers, the way a man counts who has counted before. Children, sent up with ribbons on their sleeves. Not one came home to show the Hollow what a Wright looks like. Not one letter.
>
> "That's what the Hollow gave," he says. "I'm not saying what it got back, because I'd like to be fair."
>
> Pechko, from the stove: "That's fairer than most."
>
> Věra says nothing more. She does not look at Bohdan again for the rest of the evening.

*Wrongness:* Věra wrote the letter that started this, and will neither join him nor stop him.

##### OR-Q3 · A cold supper
> Bohdan has asked your hunters to supper in the Hollow, and they go. It is a good supper: goat, barley, a sour cheese that somebody asks about twice.
>
> At dusk each house in the Hollow sets a lamp in its window, for whoever might be on the road. It is the oldest courtesy the place has. Bohdan sets lamps in three of his windows. The fourth, the one that faces the road up to the hold, stays dark.
>
> One of your hunters notices. None of them says a word.
>
> Bohdan sees them notice. "I'm not rude," he says. "I gave you supper. I'll give you supper again. I won't light you home, that's all."
>
> Nobody argues. On the road back, somebody says it was the warmest welcome and the coldest goodnight they had ever had, both at once.
>
> "That's what it is," says somebody else. "Both at once."

*Wrongness:* one window dark, and the courtesy kept everywhere else.

##### OR-Q4 · The mouth *(two endings)*
> Bohdan has come to the Hall alone this time. He stands in front of the stove with his cap in his hands.
>
> "Věra wrote it down once," he says. "Her grandmother's word. A mouth, not a hearth. I'd thought it was a figure of speech. I've stopped being sure it is."

**Variant A (the Moot is also seated):**
> "I'd like to say this to the heads," he says. "Not to you. To them."
>
> You send for them. Marta comes, then the others, grumbling, and Bohdan says it again, the same words in the same order. Nobody interrupts him.
>
> "Say it again when we're not tired," Marta says. Then, to Bohdan's evident surprise: "I'll hear it."

**Variant B (the Moot is not seated):**
> He takes a stone from his coat, a grey river stone, cold as a well, and sets it on the hearthstone in front of the stove.
>
> "It was in our spring all winter," he says. "It never warmed in my hand."
>
> He leaves it there. He does not look back at the stove as he goes.

**Ending, both variants:**
> The stove ticks. Pechko says nothing, which is rare.
>
> The question stays where it was left, in the middle of the floor, and everyone steps around it.

*Wrongness:* the argument is not settled, and nobody tries to settle it. *Threshold:* the question left in the middle of the floor.

### E2. Grey Wives: one hint per level, in the recipe Book (and an elder's note when there is nothing left to hint at)

**Rule.** At level *n* (seated), the Book shows under its grid *"The eldest Grey Wife says:"* and the level's hint, **while at least one brew named in a hint at level ≤ n is not yet in the Book**; the claim is about that brew. When the Book already holds every brew named, the hint is replaced by the level's **elder's note**. Hints name a brew and never an ingredient.

| Level | Brew named | Hint | Elder's note (when the brew is known) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Steadfast Draught | *A plain herb from the garden has a quiet use in a Steadfast Draught.* | *"A kettle remembers what was put in it. Don't scour it too clean."* |
| 2 | Owl-Eye Drops | *Look first at what the villages send. Owl-Eye Drops are in more than one of their gifts.* | *"Write down nothing you can't say aloud."* |
| 3 | Coldwater Draught | *Coldwater Draught is not one thing's work. Two unlike things carry it.* | *"Two hands. One to hold, one to give."* |
| 4 | Wright's Oil | *Wright's Oil wants what burns slowly and what lies still.* | *"Leave a cup out."* |

*(Data for the test, from GDD §8.6: Steadfast Draught is carried by mugwort, juniper and wolf fang, and mugwort and juniper come from the Herb Garden. Owl-Eye Drops by honeycomb, hare's foot and wolf fang; honeycomb and hare's foot are village gifts (two or more). Coldwater Draught has exactly two carriers, river pearl and noon poppy. Wright's Oil by pitch and grave dust: pitch is a slow-burning thing and grave dust is a drop from the dead.)* The test encodes each hint as data `{brew, claim}` and checks it against the §8.6 table.

### E3. Bell-Watchers: sharper where-to-look text in the Names view

**Rule (generated, never typed).** The Names view already shows a hint for each missing name (lore §20). While the Bell-Watchers are seated at level *n*, the missing names of **group *n*** (below) get one extra sentence after their hint, **the same sentence for every name in the group that is still missing**, built from **only the group's names that are still missing**:

- *n* = how many are missing; *y₁* and *y₂* = the first and last of their years; the villages = the distinct villages of those names.
- **Sentence:** `{Count} {springs|spring}, {year clause}, {village clause}.` with **Count** spelled out (One, Two … Ten), **year clause** = `from year {y₁} to year {y₂}` if *n* > 1, or `year {y₁}` if *n* = 1, and **village clause** = `from {A}` (1 village), `from {A} and {B}` (2), `from {A}, {B} and {C}` (3, villages in the order Dubno Ford, Kozly Hollow, Mirograd, Ravenfell, Velkvik), `from every village but {X}` (4), `from every village` (5).
- **Nothing is shown** if none of the group's names is still missing, and nothing is added for any name outside the group. It adds no name, source or document.
