# The Last Hold — Lore Bible

**Status:** Draft for review.
**Spoilers:** This file contains the whole truth of the central mystery. Claude Code reads it only when building lore content (v0.5 onward) or when naming things.
**Markers:** **[JC]** = judgment call. All proper nouns are working names until they pass the collision search (GDD §2.3).

---

## 1. Theme in one line

> **What we burn to keep ourselves safe becomes the dark we fear. What we remember keeps us warm.**

The mystery and the game's mechanics say the same thing:
- **The villains' sin is burning memory to stay safe.** The Emberwrights fed the Hearthseal with people.
- **The player's way out is remembering.** The bestiary, the recipe book, the letters, and the "Knowledge is progression" pillar (GDD §1.3) are the game's quiet answer to the order's crime.

The tone stays **heroic with dark edges** (D01):
- The dead were brave.
- The betrayers had reasons.
- Nobody is a cartoon villain, not even the thing beneath.

## 2. The world

### 2.1 The Gloamreach

A wide frontier between the southern kingdoms and the frozen north. The fog in its valleys never fully lifts. It has three bands:

| Band | Character | Folklore flavor | Villages (GDD §8.2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Lowlands** | River country, old woods, barley fields, mill-towns | Slavic | Dubno Ford, Kozly Hollow, Mirograd |
| **The Fells** | Cold highlands, fjord inlets, barrow-hills, standing stones | Nordic | Velkvik, Ravenfell |
| **The Deep** | The chasm beneath Stanholm and the rifts that open from it | Neither: older than both | — |

Stanholm stands where the lowland river meets the first fells. It is the only place where both peoples have ever agreed on anything, which is why the order was founded there.

### 2.2 The three layers of darkness

1. **Monsters** (contracts, v0.2+). Folklore creatures are real in the Gloamreach and always have been. Since the Fall, there are more of them, and they are crueler. Grief that once faded now curdles into a rusalka; a careless burial now always rises.
   - **In-world cause:** the Unlit's cold leaks up through the broken seal and deepens every lost warmth.
2. **Cursed ruins** (v0.5+). Emberwright outposts and forges abandoned on the Night of the Fall. Seal-fire went wrong in them, and their dead walk. Original creatures live here (§6).
3. **The thing beneath** (v0.5+, Delves v0.6). **The Unlit.** It is felt as cold, as forgetting, and as the dark between stars. It is never seen whole.

### 2.3 Daily life

- **The villages are tired but not broken.** They pay bounties in silver and gifts. They name children after hunters who saved them, and they argue about everything.
- **Hunters are respected and slightly feared.** That's an older Gloamreach attitude to anyone who walks toward monsters.
- **The thanes** (local headmen and headwomen) run each village. Fell villages call theirs a thane; lowland villages say headman or headwoman.
- **Religion is local and practical:** offerings to the domovoi, salt at thresholds, bells against drowning. No organized church appears in the game **[JC: avoids real-world religious analogues]**.

## 3. The Emberwrights

### 3.1 What the order was

An order of **forge-smiths who fought with fire**. It was founded about three hundred years before the game, to protect common folk from the dark.
- They forged **silvered and runed steel**. They fought with **fire**: forge-heated blades, pitch, and runes that burn.
- **Their vow:** *"The fire for the folk; the folk never for the fire."* The order's whole tragedy is that this line was eventually broken.
- **Ranks:** Apprentice → Wright → Master-Wright → the **Firstblade** (field champion) and the **Forge-Mother** or **Forge-Father** (head of the order and keeper of the Deep Forge).
- **Symbol:** an anvil with a flame rising from it in place of a hammer.
- **In the game,** the order is why the Smithy matters: silvered and runed gear, Emberwright Plate, and (v0.5) runed weapons with Rune damage.

### 3.2 The Hearthseal

Beneath Stanholm is the **Deep Forge**, a natural chasm where the founders found that the Unlit recoils from forge-fire. Over it they built the **Hearthseal**: a vast runed hearth that burns permanently and holds the Unlit down with heat.

**The founding secret:** ordinary fire wasn't enough. The Hearthseal burns **embers**, which is what the order called the distilled warmth of a life: its memories, its loves, the meaning it carried.
- At the founding, every Emberwright swore to give their own ember to the seal at death.
- This was the order's noble, terrible heart. **"We are the fuel."**

**The founder:** **Askel Vel**, a fell-born smith. He married a lowland smith and joined their two forges. He was the first to give his ember. He becomes the patron **the Pale Smith** (§5.6).

## 4. The central mystery: the full truth

### 4.1 What actually happened (chronological)

1. **The founding (~300 years ago).** Askel Vel and the first Wrights build Stanholm and the Hearthseal. For a century and more, dead Emberwrights' embers are enough.
2. **The thinning (~120 years ago).** War in the south and plague in the lowlands shrink the order. Fewer Wrights die in old age at the hold; more die far away, and their embers are lost. The seal starts to falter.
3. **The Tithe (~100 years ago).** A Forge-Father, to keep the seal burning, starts **taking apprentices** from the villages:
   - One child from every village every few years, celebrated as an honor.
   - The villages believe the apprentices go on to distant forges.
   - **In truth, they are carried down into the Deep Forge and given to the seal.**
   - The ledger records them as *"kindled."* Only the Forge-Mother or Forge-Father and a handful of Master-Wrights know.
4. **Dobrava Halsten becomes Forge-Mother (~40 years ago).** She inherits the Tithe and hates it. She tries for twenty years to find another fuel and fails. **She keeps the Tithe running, because the alternative is the Unlit loose in the Gloamreach.** *Few to save many.*
5. **The apprentice who ran (~22 years ago).** An apprentice named **Sigrun** (later *Halfhand*) sees the kindling and flees. Seal-fire takes her hand. She hides in the fells.
6. **Yrsa Varn learns the truth (~20 years ago).** The Firstblade, the order's champion and the villages' favorite, finds her younger brother **Eirik Varn**'s name in the kindling ledger. He was taken as an apprentice eight years earlier.
7. **The Night of the Fall (~20 years ago).**
   - Yrsa and a handful of Wrights loyal to her go down into the Deep Forge to **break the tithe-mechanism**: the rune-chains that draw embers from living people.
   - She succeeds. The seal, no longer fed, gutters.
   - **The Unlit rises.** Stanholm falls in a single night. Most of the order dies, and the villages see the sky go black over the hold.
8. **Dobrava's last act.** As the hold burns, Dobrava has to choose.
   - She could restart the Tithe-chains with the surviving Wrights' embers. She doesn't. **Some of her refusal is guilt, and some of it is mercy.**
   - Instead, she **reforges the seal around one person**: she binds it to the office of **warden of Stanholm**. Whoever holds the Hearth Hall becomes the seal's keeper.
   - When the hold falls (the **Last Stand**), the warden's ember is drawn into the seal. It burns long enough to hold the Unlit down, and the tempered remnant is **rekindled into the next warden**. That next warden carries what the last one knew.
   - **Dobrava was the first warden.** She burned all of the Tithe records she could reach, so the order's crime would die with it. *(This is the "Forge-Mother let the hold fall to bury the secret" from the outline. It was true, but not the whole truth.)*
9. **The wardens' cycle (20 years to now).** Each warden rebuilds Stanholm, pushes back the dark, makes the Last Stand, and is rekindled. **The player is the latest warden.** The **Embers** prestige currency is exactly what it sounds like: the tempered warmth of every warden before you.

### 4.2 The deepest layer (revealed last)

**The Hearthseal and the Unlit are one loop.**
- Every ember burned in the seal leaves **ash**: warmth turned to cold and memory turned to forgetting.
- That ash sinks into the Deep and **is the Unlit.** The order didn't find the Unlit beneath Stanholm. A small cold thing was there. **Three hundred years of burning grew it into the Unlit.**
- So the Tithe made the problem worse, and the warden cycle still does, only slowly.
- **Only the Unlit knows this.** Askel Vel's ember (the Pale Smith) half-suspects it. Yrsa learns it too late, in the Deep after the Fall.

**What the Unlit is.** It isn't evil. It is the cold that remains when someone has been burned and forgotten. **It wants to be remembered.** It doesn't want to be fed, which is why every new ember makes it worse.

### 4.3 Who betrayed whom

| Who | The betrayal | Their reason | How the story treats them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yrsa Varn** (the Firstblade) | Broke the seal and caused the Fall | Her brother, and every tithed child | A **hero who made a catastrophic choice**. She was right about the crime and wrong about what would follow. |
| **Dobrava Halsten** (Forge-Mother) | Kept the Tithe; buried the truth | Few to save many; then shame | A **tragic keeper**. She paid for it by becoming the first warden. |
| **The order itself** | Broke its vow: "the folk never for the fire" | Fear of the dark | The real fall happened a century before the Night |
| **Askel Vel** (the founder) | Built a seal that burns people (willingly, at first) | He didn't know about the ash | The **original mistake**, made in good faith |

The mystery's shape follows the brief's request for **a great betrayal**: something fell from within, and each cycle shows another point of view. It's an original story.
- There is no single traitor. There is a chain of people choosing safety over the folk.
- Each cycle's witness believes they know who the traitor was.

### 4.5 The final act (draft)

At the last Last Stand, the warden can choose:

1. **Keep the Vigil.** Continue the cycle. The game becomes an endless mode with rising difficulty and a bittersweet ending text.
2. **Let the Fire Go Out.** Refuse to be rekindled. The seal ends, and the Unlit rises fully. There is one final battle, using everything learned across every cycle: the bestiary, relics, the roster, and every warden's ember spent at once.
   - **Win:** the Unlit is scattered, not destroyed. A heroic, costly ending.
   - **Lose:** the Gloamreach goes dark, and the cycle starts over.
3. **Remember Them.** *Requires a complete bestiary and every Tithe name recovered from the documents* **[JC]**.
   - The warden reads the names of every kindled apprentice and every burned Wright into the Hearth Hall, and the villages remember them.
   - The Unlit, finally remembered, grows warm and small again.
   - The seal can go out safely. **This is the true ending**, and it is earned through the knowledge pillar.

### 4.6 Rules for writing the mystery

- **Every document is from someone's point of view,** and every point of view is partly wrong.
- **The UI never says which account is true.** The Archivist's *Accounts* view lines up conflicting versions of the same event side by side (§7.3).
- **Each act must work on its own,** with its own small closure, in case the player stops there.
- **No document requires a Delve to understand the plot** (D20). Delve-only documents add depth, never plot-critical facts.
- **The Tithe names** are scattered across documents in every act: about 40 names in total. Recovering them is the requirement for the true ending, and the Archivist tracks progress.

## 8. Naming and originality checklist (lore)

- **Collision-search every name** in this file before v0.5, including Gloamreach, Stanholm, Emberwright, Hearthseal, the Unlit, Firstblade, and every character. Log any collisions in `decisions.md`.
- **The Warhammer-flavored elements** (an order of fire-smiths; a betrayal from within) appear as *structure only*. No names, ranks, or imagery from that setting:
  - No power armor.
  - No "chapter," "primarch," "heresy," or "legion."
  - No salamander or dragon iconography for the order.
- **Hades-flavored elements** (patron spirits, a story that advances on each reset): patrons are the dead of this world's own story, not gods. Blessings are "blessings," never "boons."
- **Witcher-flavored elements:** monsters and contracts follow folklore and this world's history. No mutated hunter caste; hunters here are ordinary brave people.

## Judgment calls in the lore bible

1. **The order burned memory ("embers") to fuel the seal,** first willingly and later through the Tithe.
2. **The prestige loop is the seal,** and Embers are literally previous wardens. Dobrava was the first warden.
3. **The deepest twist:** the seal's ash *is* the Unlit, so the solution fed the problem.
4. **Six acts across six cycles** (about 3–4 weeks of play), one witness per act, plus a final act whose timing is still open.
5. **Three endings.** The true ending, *Remember Them*, requires a complete bestiary and every recovered Tithe name.
6. **The patrons are the dead of the story:** the Pale Smith (founder), the Wolf Beneath the Hill (Yrsa), the Hearth-Crone (Dobrava), and the Drowned Bell (the kindled).
7. **Yrsa as a possible legendary hunter** in a Legendary Hunt, and **Arvid as a later hunter.**
8. **Old Ostap half-remembers earlier wardens** because the domovoi shares the stove's warmth with him.
9. **No organized religion** appears in the game.
10. **Document texts are written just before each act's milestone,** not all at once now.
11. **"The Forgotten"** grow stronger the less of the bestiary you've filled in.
12. **The voice sample (Halina's letter)** sets the writing standard: plain, specific, and one wrongness at a time.
